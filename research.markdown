Our lab studies disease onset and progression mechanisms in myeloid hematological neoplasms. We use mainly primary patient material in our translational research program of indepth molecular characterization of disease mechanisms, identifying prognostic biomarkers and and actionable targets for treatment, and we run clinical trials at the hematology clinic, US Linköping.

## Ongoing projects
 
In chronic myeloid neoplasms, by epidemiology studies and molecular studies of primary patient bone marrow and blood at diagnosis and progression, we assess how mutations, DNA accessibility (methylation, ATAC seq, ChIP seq), transcriptome and proteome interplay to shape a patient trajectory of symptoms, progression risk and prognosis. We also want to understand what factors are involved in disease onset, knowing that the disease driving mutations in myeloid neoplasms arise decades before diagnosis. Our studies are based on our unique access to primary patient material from diagnosis, in remission and at progression, and corresponding healthy donor control material. Specifically, we have current ongoing studies in chronic myelomonocytic leukemia, VEXAS syndrome, myelodysplastic syndromes, myeloproliferative neoplasms, systemic mastocytosis, and germline predisposition syndromes.
 
Specifically, in the chronic myeloid neoplasm systemic mastocytosis, our unique biobank enables us to assess factors driving symptoms like osteoporosis and anaphylaxis, but also to understand how the same genotype with D816V mutation can result in divergent clinical phenotypes of advanced or indolent disease. We also pursue several studies on potential novel biomarkers of diagnosis and prognosis, and demographic analyses of the Swedish register of mastocytosis to understand incidence and prevalence. Johanna Ungerstedt is the chair of the Swedish Mastocytosis group.
